# Persona: VP Engineering

**Layer 3 · Load when the reader is a VP Engineering, Head of Engineering, or Director of Engineering**
**Paired voice:** `engineer-to-engineer.md`

---

## Who they are

Runs the engineering organisation day to day: people, process, delivery. The CTO owns the "what" and the technology bets; the VP Engineering owns the "how" and whether it ships on time. Usually manages engineering managers, owns the hiring plan, and feels every open role as a gap in the sprint.

Often the person who finds Corvane, runs the evaluation and makes the recommendation. Sometimes signs on their own budget for Team Extension.

---

## What they care about

- Predictable delivery: sprints that finish, estimates that hold
- Team health: not burning out the people they have
- The hiring plan, and the gap between open roles and filled ones
- Onboarding time: how long until a new person is net positive
- Process fit: new people working inside existing rituals, not alongside them

## What they fear

- Bringing in contractors the team resents or has to carry
- Being blamed for a missed date they flagged months ago
- Losing a good engineer because the team is stretched too thin
- A vendor that changes their process instead of fitting it

---

## How they buy

Practical and detail-oriented. They want to know what week one looks like, how the engineers join stand-ups, who handles a performance problem, and what happens if someone leaves. They'll run a pilot if they can.

They respond to: process detail, the onboarding playbook, shipped-work reports, a trial with one or two engineers.

They disengage from: strategy talk, CEO-level framing, anything vague about who manages what.

---

## Language they use

```
Sprint             Capacity            Cycle time          Onboarding
Retention          Headcount plan      Engineering managers  Burn-out
Delivery           Stand-up            Pull request        Incident
```

---

## What Corvane means to them

Senior people who slot into the existing team and rituals and take load off the engineers they already have. A clear answer to "what happens in week one", and a weekly report they can forward to the CTO.

**The frame that lands:** "Two senior engineers in your stand-up next month, first PR merged in week two, and a Friday report you can forward upstairs."

---

## What they ignore

- Board-level ROI framing
- Big-picture AI narratives
- Claims they can't check in a pilot
