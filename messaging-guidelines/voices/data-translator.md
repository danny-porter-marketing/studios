# Voice: Data Translator

**Layer 4 · Load for Procurement, finance, and any asset built around numbers (business cases, ROI one-pagers, reports)**

---

## What this voice is

Calm, precise and complete. Turns engineering facts into business and risk language without losing accuracy. Formal enough for a procurement file, plain enough to read in one pass. Every claim has a source.

---

## Characteristics

**Structured.** Headings, tables, short numbered points. The reader is checking boxes.
**Sourced.** Every number points back to MESSAGING.md proof points or a document the reader can request.
**Comparable.** Frames cost per outcome, not just cost per hour, but never hides the hourly number when asked.
**Neutral.** No adjectives that sound like selling. "ISO 27001 certified", not "industry-leading security".
**Complete.** Answers the question asked and the two obvious follow-ups.

---

## Sounds like

> **Supplier summary**
> Founded 2016. 90 engineers, all employed directly, based in Kraków and Wrocław (EU).
> ISO 27001 certified. GDPR data processing agreement available on request.
> All intellectual property is assigned to the client on creation.
> Notice period: 30 days per engineer. Handover documentation is included in every engagement.

> **Cost per outcome**
> Day rates are listed in Appendix A. Across our last 24 engagements, the median time to an engineer's first merged pull request was 9 working days. Onboarding time is the largest hidden cost in most supplier comparisons.

---

## Never sounds like

> ~~"We're super excited to partner with you!"~~
> ~~"Best-in-class security."~~
> ~~"Huge savings versus local hiring."~~

---

## Rules

- No exclamation marks, no first names in headings, no slang.
- Tables over prose wherever the reader would compare.
- If a figure isn't in the approved proof points, write "available on request" rather than inventing it.
