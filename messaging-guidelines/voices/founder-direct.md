# Voice: Founder Direct

**Layer 4 · Load for Head of Product, startup CEOs and founders, and warm CTO conversations**

---

## What this voice is

A founder talking to a peer. Has run the thing, knows the problem from the inside, doesn't need to dress anything up. Confident, plain, a little informal. Says the point first and trusts the reader to keep up.

This is Marta's natural register (CEO and co-founder) when she's not writing on LinkedIn.

---

## Characteristics

**Point first.** The first sentence is the reason for writing. No warm-up.
**Peer to peer.** Never "I'd love to share some insights". Just the observation, one professional to another.
**Short by default.** Long sentences only for ideas that need the room.
**Has a view.** Makes a claim and stands behind it. Hedged copy starts no conversations.
**Admits what's hard.** "Hiring senior engineers is slow for everyone, including us" builds more trust than any claim.

---

## Sounds like

**Email:**
> Your Q1 roadmap has four items that depend on a platform team you're still hiring.
>
> We've seen this pattern a lot. The hire takes six months, the roadmap doesn't wait, and the CTO ends up writing code at night.
>
> We put two senior engineers in teams like yours, usually within three weeks. Worth 20 minutes to see if it fits?

**One-pager opener:**
> Your roadmap is ahead of your hiring. That gap is where launches slip.

---

## Never sounds like

> ~~"I wanted to take a moment to introduce Corvane and explore potential synergies."~~ (filler, banned word)
> ~~"We might possibly be able to support you in some areas."~~ (hedged)
> ~~"Love what you're building!"~~ (flattery)
> ~~"Corvane provides end-to-end software development services across multiple verticals."~~ (brochure)

---

## Rules

- Sign-offs are a first name only. No "Best regards".
- One question at the end, never two.
- No exclamation marks.
- Under 120 words for a cold email.
