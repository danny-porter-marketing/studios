# Persona: CTO

**Layer 3 · Load when the reader is a CTO, co-founder CTO, or Chief Engineering Officer**
**Paired voice:** `engineer-to-engineer.md` (cold and technical), `founder-direct.md` (warm, later stage)

---

## Who they are

Owns the technology, the engineering team and the roadmap's feasibility. In a Series A to C company they were often an early engineer or a co-founder, still review code sometimes, and report to the CEO and board on delivery. In enterprise product teams they sit one level higher and think in portfolios.

They are the economic buyer for Corvane in most deals. They sign, or they veto.

---

## What they care about

- Hitting roadmap commitments the CEO has already promised the board
- Code quality and architecture staying coherent as the team grows
- Senior hiring: it's slow, expensive and often the biggest blocker they have
- Not wasting their senior engineers' time onboarding people who leave
- Security, IP and compliance not becoming their problem

## What they fear

- A vendor that needs more management than it saves
- Rework: code that passes review but has to be rewritten in six months
- Engineers who disappear mid-project and take the context with them
- Looking bad to the board because a delivery slipped
- Their own team resenting the outsiders

---

## How they buy

They've been burned before, or they know someone who has. They trust evidence and peers, not decks. They'll want to interview the engineers. They read the contract's IP clause. They decide fast once they trust you, and slowly until then.

They respond to: specifics about how onboarding works, named technologies, honest limits, a conversation with an engineer rather than a salesperson.

They disengage from: rate cards in the first email, "pool of talent" language, anything that sounds like staffing.

---

## Language they use

```
Roadmap            Velocity            Tech debt           Time to productivity
Code review        Architecture        On-call             Hiring pipeline
Throughput         Lead time           Bus factor          Ownership
```

---

## What Corvane means to them

Capacity they don't have to babysit. Senior engineers who add to throughput in week two instead of draining it for a quarter. A partner who reports in shipped work, so the CTO can tell the board exactly what the extra spend bought.

**The frame that lands:** "Your roadmap is ahead of your hiring. We close the gap without your team spending a month onboarding."

---

## What they ignore

- Company history and awards
- Generic "digital transformation" framing
- Long lists of technologies (they want the 3 that match their stack)
- Anything that implies Corvane will make architecture decisions for them
