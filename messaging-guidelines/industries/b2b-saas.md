# Industry: B2B SaaS

**Load when the account sells software to businesses on subscription**

---

## What's different about selling here

- **The roadmap is the sales pitch.** Sales has promised features to close deals. Engineering capacity decides whether those promises hold.
- **Legacy architecture is common.** Many Series B and C SaaS companies are still on the monolith they launched with, and need to modernise without stopping feature work.
- **"Add AI" pressure.** Boards and customers are asking. Most teams have no one who has shipped an LLM feature.

## Common stacks

Ruby on Rails, Node and TypeScript, Python and Django, React, PostgreSQL, AWS. Increasingly OpenAI or Anthropic APIs for new AI features.

## Pains to lead with

- Feature commitments made in sales deals that engineering can't deliver this quarter
- A monolith slowing every release
- Board pressure for AI features with no in-house experience
- Churn driven by missing integrations

## Proof to use

Northrow case study: migration from a Rails monolith to services, zero unplanned downtime over 7 months.

## Words to use

Roadmap, release cadence, churn, integrations, migration, monolith, customer commitments.

## Avoid

- Implying their architecture is bad. They know. Talk about the path forward.
